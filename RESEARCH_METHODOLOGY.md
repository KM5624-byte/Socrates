# Research methodology

The pilot chart displays published survey or program estimates. Policy events supply context. New York County represents Manhattan; coverage is narrower than the law's jurisdiction. National series have different populations, and the three BLS series also differ in survey or seasonal adjustment. Same-month overlap alone supplies no treatment identification.

The proposed 2023–2026 Local Law 144 study asks how accessibility, completeness and empirical usefulness evolve. Define the unit as a captured audit-summary version, with separate dates for audit, original publication where known, and AILEO capture. A prospective capture protocol and date-specific search coverage are required before inference about trends.

For each review capture, obtain an evidence URL, page or section locator, reviewer, capture date and evidence hash where permitted. Evaluate document accessibility, identified employer, tool/vendor, audit date, methodology, demographics reported and source-result fields. Classify each attribute as present, absent after review, unavailable, or not reviewed. Keep inaccessible captures in the denominator for accessibility and exclude them from field-completeness denominators. Report denominators and scope per field. Do not infer employer-wide coverage or discrimination from missing documents. Audit disclosure results are pending corpus acquisition and review.

The review protocol has executable data contracts and denominator checks in [research/disclosures.py](research/disclosures.py). The [policy coverage notebook](research/notebooks/policy_coverage.py) checks NYC milestone overlap on a pinned LAUS release and records unsupported identification when coverage and comparison are inadequate.
